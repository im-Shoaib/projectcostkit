Brick Calculator: Bricks and Mortar for a Wall

Work out bricks and 80 lb mortar bags for a wall with the modular rate, openings, waste and a full worked example.

A modular brick wall takes 6.75 bricks per sq ft with standard 3/8 in mortar joints, so a 100 sq ft wall needs 675 bricks before waste. This brick calculator works out the full order: net wall area, bricks with waste, and 80 lb bags of mortar. The steps below show the same math by hand, so you can check any quote.

The formula

Net wall area (sq ft) = (length in ft x height in ft) - openings in sq ft

Bricks needed = net wall area x bricks per sq ft for your brick size

Bricks to order = bricks needed x 1.10 for 10 percent waste

Mortar bags = bricks to order / bricks laid per bag, rounded up

The Brick Industry Association (BIA) lists 675 modular bricks per 100 sq ft with 3/8 in joints in Technical Note 10, Table 4. That is 6.75 per sq ft, or about 7 for a quick check. A modular brick measures 3 5/8 x 2 1/4 x 7 5/8 in. Add one 3/8 in joint and it fills an 8 x 2 5/8 in patch of wall.

Brick size Actual size, D x H x L (in) Bricks per sq ft, 3/8 in joint
Modular 3 5/8 x 2 1/4 x 7 5/8 6.75
Queen 2 3/4 x 2 3/4 x 7 5/8 5.50
Norman 3 5/8 x 2 1/4 x 11 5/8 4.50
Utility 3 5/8 x 3 5/8 x 11 5/8 3.00

For mortar, bag labels are the safest guide. Sakrete lists its 80 lb (36.3 kg) Type N bag at 35 standard bricks per bag. Quikrete lists its 80 lb Mortar Mix at up to 37. Both skip waste, so plan on the low end.

Worked example: a 20 by 8 ft wall with one window

Say you are building a single wythe wall, 20 ft long and 8 ft high (6.1 x 2.4 m). Single wythe means one brick thick, in running bond. There is one window, 5 ft wide by 4 ft high. You are using modular brick and 80 lb bags of mortar.

Step 1, gross wall area: 20 ft x 8 ft = 160 sq ft.

Step 2, subtract the opening: 5 ft x 4 ft = 20 sq ft. Net wall area = 160 - 20 = 140 sq ft.

Step 3, bricks for the net area: 140 sq ft x 6.75 bricks per sq ft = 945 bricks.

Step 4, add waste: 945 x 1.10 = 1,039.5 bricks. Round up like a buyer to 1,040 bricks.

Step 5, mortar bags: 1,040 bricks / 35 bricks per 80 lb bag = 29.7 bags. Round up to 30 bags.

Check the math a second way. At about 7 bricks per sq ft, 140 sq ft x 7 = 980 bricks before waste. That sits close to the 945 figure above. The gap is the rounding in the quick rule. For the order, stick with 1,040 bricks and 30 bags.

How do you measure a wall for brick?

Measure length and height in feet, wall by wall. For a gable or sloped top, split the shape into a rectangle plus a triangle and add the two areas. Our square footage calculator handles L-shapes, circles and triangles.

Measure every door and window opening and subtract it. Use the masonry opening, from brick to brick, not the glass size. Openings under 1 sq ft are often left in the count, since the brick around them gets cut anyway.

Write down brick size and joint width while you measure. A wall figured at 6.75 bricks per sq ft will come up short if the yard ships queen or utility brick and you keep the old rate. Check the size on the cube strap, not just the name the salesperson used.

What changes the brick and mortar count?

Wall thickness is the big one. The rates above are for a single wythe wall. A double wythe wall takes twice the brick and close to twice the mortar for the same face area. This one choice can double the bill.

Joint width matters too. Open the joint from 3/8 in to 1/2 in and the wall takes fewer bricks, since each brick covers more wall, but more mortar per 1,000 bricks. Keep the joint the plans call for.

Bond pattern changes cuts and waste. Plain running bond is the low waste case at about 10 percent. Patterns with lots of headers, such as Flemish bond, need more cuts. The BIA says to add at least 5 percent for brick waste and 15 to 25 percent for mortar waste.

Brick pavers are a different job, since most patio work has no mortar. For flatwork, use our paver calculator instead of the wall rates here.

Mistakes that leave you short

The most common error is ordering from gross area. Forget one 20 sq ft window and you pay for about 135 bricks you do not need.

The second error is mixing up actual and nominal size. A modular brick is 7 5/8 in long, but with its joint it lays out at 8 in. Divide the wall by the bare brick length and you will order about 20 percent too many bricks.

The third error is trusting the top mortar yield on the bag. That 35 to 37 bricks per bag figure allows nothing for waste or dropped mortar. Figuring bags from your full brick order, as in the worked example, covers part of that. Keep one or two spare bags on site anyway.

Last, order all your brick from one batch. Clay color shifts from one kiln run to the next, and a two batch wall can show a clear band where the color changed.

When should you hire a mason?

A low garden wall, a mailbox pier or a short planter is fair DIY work if the base is solid and level. Keep joints at 3/8 in, and tool them when the mortar is thumbprint hard.

Call a mason for anything structural: load bearing walls, veneer over a house frame, lintels over wide openings, chimneys, and walls that hold back soil. Tall walls need ties, flashing and weep holes placed right, and many areas need a permit for masonry over a set height.

Frequently asked questions

How many bricks are in 100 sq ft of wall?

For modular brick with 3/8 in joints, the BIA rate is 675 bricks per 100 sq ft, single wythe. Add 10 percent for waste and order about 743 bricks. Double wythe takes double that.

How many bags of mortar do I need for 1,000 bricks?

At 35 bricks per 80 lb bag, 1,000 bricks take 28.6 bags, so round up to 29 bags before waste. With waste allowed for, plan on about 32 to 33 bags.

How much waste should I add for brick?

Add 10 percent for a plain running bond wall with few cuts. The BIA floor is at least 5 percent, and complex patterns or lots of openings call for 15 percent. Keep a few spares from the same batch for repairs.

What is the standard modular brick size?

A modular brick measures 3 5/8 x 2 1/4 x 7 5/8 in (92 x 57 x 194 mm). With a 3/8 in joint its nominal size is 4 x 2 2/3 x 8 in, and three courses rise exactly 8 in.

Is a brick wall the same as a concrete block wall?

No. A standard 8 x 16 in block covers far more wall per unit, at 1.125 blocks per sq ft. If your wall is block, use our concrete block calculator for block, mortar and core fill counts.

What mortar type should I use for a brick wall?

Type N is the common pick for above grade walls and veneer. Sakrete rates its Type N mix above 750 psi. Type S is stronger and suits walls in contact with soil or below grade work.
