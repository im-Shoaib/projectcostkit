Angi's 2026 data puts the average cost to replace a roof at $9,609, with a wide range from $5,903 on a small simple job to $46,000 on a large premium one. Per square foot, Angi puts replacement at $4 to $11 for most homes. HomeGuide gives a similar picture by the square: $400 to $900 per square (100 sq ft) installed for a standard shingle replacement, with a national average total near $10,800 and a common range of $5,700 to $16,000. Material alone, before labor, runs $80 to $130 per square for 3-tab shingles and $100 to $250 per square for architectural shingles, per HomeGuide. Metal material runs $100 to $800 per square, tile $300 to $2,500 per square, and slate $600 to $1,600 per square. Labor to install shingles runs $200 to $350 per square. That split is why two quotes on the same house can differ by thousands: one roofer may price a basic 3-tab, the other a thick architectural shingle with new flashing and vents.

DIY versus pro is a short table on a roof, and we will be blunt about it:

Route What you pay for Typical cost on 17 squares
Pro tear-off and replace Material, labor, tear-off, disposal, warranty $6,800 to $15,300
DIY material only Shingles, underlayment, nails, flashing, dumpster $2,500 to $4,500

The DIY column looks cheap until you price your own safety gear, your weekends, and the fact that most shingle warranties want a correct install with the right nail pattern and ventilation. A roof is the one job where a small leak can damage drywall, insulation and floors below. If you are not sure, hire it out. Worked example: a 17 square architectural shingle roof

Take a roof with 1,700 sq ft of sloped surface, which is 17 squares. The old roof is one layer of shingles. The owner picks architectural shingles, the mid-range choice on most streets.

Step 1, low price: 17 squares x $400 per square = $6,800.

Step 2, typical price: 17 squares x $650 per square = $11,050.

Step 3, high price: 17 squares x $900 per square = $15,300.

Step 4, check the per square build-up. HomeGuide breaks one square into tear-off $100 to $300, material $100 to $250 and labor $200 to $350. The low ends add to $400, the high ends add to $900, so the table and the build-up agree. We ran the totals both ways, per square foot and per square, and they match: $4 to $9 per sq ft on this build-up, inside Angi's $4 to $11 range.

Step 5, extras to budget on top. Two sheets of bad decking, new ridge vent and a permit can add $400 to $1,200 on a job this size. Ask for decking priced per sheet in the written quote, so a soft spot found at tear-off does not turn into an open-ended charge.

So the planning budget for this house is $6,800 low, $11,050 typical and $15,300 high, plus a small cushion for decking. That range sits right on Angi's $9,609 average, which is what you would expect for a mid-size shingle job.

What pushes the price up or down?

Material is the biggest lever. Asphalt shingles are the low-cost standard. Standing seam metal costs two to three times as much installed but lasts 40 years or more against 20 to 30 years for architectural shingles. Tile and slate sit at the top on price and weight, and some houses need stronger framing before they can carry them. If a quote for metal or tile looks close to a shingle quote, ask what was left out.

Tear-off is the second lever. One layer is the normal case and is in the per square prices above. A second layer adds removal labor and dumpster weight. Some codes allow a roof-over with no tear-off, which saves money now, but it hides deck problems and can shorten the life of the new shingles. Most good roofers advise a full tear-off so they can see the deck.

Pitch and shape add labor. Steep roofs over 7/12 need roof jacks, harnesses and slower work, and Angi notes steep roofs can cost 15 to 50 percent more. Valleys, hips, dormers, skylights and chimneys each add flashing work and cutting time. A plain gable roof on a ranch is the cheapest shape per square. A two-story house with a cut-up roof is the most dear.

Region and timing matter too. Coastal cities and the Northeast run high on labor. Rural areas run lower. Prices also rise after a big storm season in an area, when every crew is booked for months. If your roof can wait for a quiet season, quotes often soften.

How do you get a fair quote?

Get three written quotes and make each roofer price the same job. The quote should list squares, shingle brand and line, underlayment type, tear-off layers, flashing, vents, decking price per sheet, disposal and permit. If one quote is a single line with one number, you cannot compare it and you should not sign it.

Ask what the warranty covers. There are two warranties: the maker warranty on the shingles and the roofer workmanship warranty on the install. Get both in writing, with the years stated. A long shingle warranty means little if the workmanship warranty is one year and the crew is gone in two.

Check the basics before you pay a deposit. License, insurance, a real address and recent local jobs you can drive past. Never pay the full price up front. A common pattern is a small deposit, a payment when material lands, and the balance when the job is done and cleaned up. Ways to save without cutting corners

Keep the roof you have longer with small repairs if the shingles still have life. A few lifted tabs, one bad vent boot or a short flashing leak is a repair, not a replacement. Replace when curling, bald patches and leaks show in several places, or when the roof is near the end of its rated life.

Pick architectural shingles over luxury lines if the budget is tight. You keep the 20 to 30 year life and give up some thickness and color depth. Skip the roof-over shortcut, since it saves tear-off money now and can cost you a deck later.

Do the easy parts yourself if your roofer agrees. Some owners handle yard cleanup, old gutter removal or attic insulation top-up after the roof is on. Do not offer to do the tear-off yourself to save money. Crews price tear-off as part of a fast, safe system, and a half-stripped roof in the rain is a bad trade.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to replace a 2,000 sq ft roof?

At $400 to $900 per square installed, a 2,000 sq ft roof is 20 squares and costs $8,000 to $18,000, with a typical price near $13,000 for architectural shingles.

What is the average roof replacement cost in 2026?

Angi puts the average at $9,609 in 2026, with most jobs from $5,903 to $46,000. HomeGuide puts its national average near $10,800, with a common range of $5,700 to $16,000.

How much does tear-off add to a roof replacement?

HomeGuide puts old roof removal at $100 to $300 per square. On a 17 square roof that is $1,700 to $5,100 for one layer. A second layer adds more labor and disposal cost.

Is a metal roof worth the extra cost?

It can be, if you plan to stay put. Metal material runs $100 to $800 per square against $100 to $250 for architectural shingles, but metal lasts 30 to 80 years per HomeGuide, against 20 to 30 years for shingles.

Do I need a permit to replace a roof?

In most US towns, yes. A full replacement usually needs a building permit and an inspection, and your roofer normally pulls it. Ask to see the permit before work starts.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most single-family shingle jobs take 1 to 3 days of work once the crew starts. Weather, deck repairs and a complex roof shape can stretch that to a week.
